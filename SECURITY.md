# Reporting a vulnerability

Email **security@acemq.com** with what you found and how to reproduce it. Please do
not open a public issue for anything exploitable.

You should get an acknowledgement within two working days, and an assessment of
whether it is a vulnerability, what is affected, and a rough timeline within a week.
If a fix is warranted, we will tell you when it is released and credit you unless you
would rather we did not.

## What is in scope

These are examples, so most of what is worth reporting here is a bug in the library
they demonstrate. Report those against
[acemq-python-amqp](https://github.com/AceMQ-Company/acemq-python-amqp) — including
anything found by running an example in this repository.

What belongs here rather than there:

- An example that would teach somebody an unsafe pattern: verification turned off, a
  credential in a URL, a development certificate used as if it were a real one, a key
  committed to the repository.
- An example that writes something outside its own directory, or leaves a private key
  readable by more than its owner.
- Anything in CI that would let a pull request from a fork reach a secret. There are
  no secrets in this repository today, and a change that needs one is a change worth
  looking at twice.
- A dependency pinned here that resolves to something other than what it names —
  including anything about the way `requirements.txt` names
  <https://acemq.org/pypi/simple/> alongside PyPI.

## What is not

- **Certificates under `certs/`.** They are generated by
  `python -m acemq_amqp.devcerts`, they carry
  `ACEMQ DEVELOPMENT ONLY - DO NOT TRUST` in their subject organisation, every AceMQ
  library refuses them unless explicitly told not to, and they are in `.gitignore`.
  A certificate whose private key sits beside it is not a secret and is not treated
  as one.
- **`guest:guest` in a broker URL.** It is the default login of a RabbitMQ container
  that only accepts connections from localhost. Reaching a real broker is the
  subject of `advanced/02-development-certificates`.
- **Vulnerabilities in aio-pika, cryptography, protobuf, fastavro or PyYAML** —
  report those upstream.
- **Vulnerabilities in RabbitMQ itself** — report those to Broadcom.
- Findings from a scanner with no demonstrated impact.

## Supported versions

Nothing here is released or versioned; there is nothing to tag. The main branch is
what exists, and a fix means a commit on it.
